## The insert, verbatim (Typst register, for body.typ between the Question 3 and Question 8 sections)

```
= Question 4: harmonization that stays checkable

The Commissions ask whether the SEC should more fully harmonize its SBS
reporting rules with the CFTC's swap reporting rules and, if so, "what
measures should the Commissions undertake to ensure that reporting rules
remain harmonized over time?"#note_ref(1) I answer the second half, where
harmonization fails quietly. Equivalence between two reporting frameworks
is a claim about rules, not about sampled outcomes: rulebooks that agree
on every examined case can differ on the unexamined ones, and reliance
extends to exactly those. The notice's own predicate --- whether one
framework's rules are "wholly consistent with" the other's --- becomes
checkable in one form: an obligation-level crosswalk with conformance
cases --- each obligation paired with its counterpart and with the fields
and records that discharge both, under test cases that deliberately
include the cases the two frameworks are known to treat differently.
Without the cases the determination is an assertion; with them it is a
control anyone can re-run. The determination must also be versioned,
dated, and paired with a re-examination trigger: when either framework's
rule changes, it is stale until re-made. The request supplies the
cautionary example --- on its account, reporting has been harmonized in
practice through reliance on a compliance statement now scheduled to
expire; a determination with no version, no date, and no trigger
reproduces that situation on a longer clock. On Question 4a's
specification half: an identical technical specification, where adopted,
is the cheapest way to stay harmonized, because divergence surfaces as a
visible conflict at the moment of change rather than a discovery years
later; where the specifications must differ, the difference should be an
enumerated exception carried in the specification itself, not an unstated
delta each firm reconstructs. This is the apparatus the Question 19
section already describes; one apparatus serves both. Whether the SEC
should undertake the harmonization at all is a judgment about two
rulebooks I have not compared, and I do not make it; nothing here
proposes that compliance with one framework excuse compliance with the
other.
```

## The summary edits, verbatim

The summary sentence (body.typ, "Summary of positions") becomes:

```
Questions 3, 4, 8, and 19, and take no position on the other questions.
```

replacing "Questions 3, 8, and 19, and take no position on the other
questions." Appended to the numbered position list as position 7, so that
no existing position number or "(Position n)" cross-reference moves:

```
7. Equivalence between two reporting frameworks is a claim about rules,
   not about sampled outcomes. A harmonization determination should take
   the durable form: an obligation-level crosswalk with conformance
   cases, versioned, dated, and paired with a re-examination trigger ---
   and where a technical specification is shared, identical is the
   cheapest way to stay harmonized, with any necessary difference carried
   as an enumerated exception in the specification itself.
```

Appended to "Specific requests" as item 6:

```
6. Establish harmonization as a versioned determination: an
   obligation-level crosswalk, conformance cases that include the known
   points of difference, and a stated re-examination trigger on rule
   change; where specifications are shared, carry any difference as an
   enumerated exception in the specification. (Position 7.)
```

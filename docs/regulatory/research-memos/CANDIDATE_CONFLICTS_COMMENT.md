## The candidate comment, verbatim

Re: Conflicts of Interest and Affiliations; Notice of Proposed Rulemaking,
RIN 3038-AF76, 91 FR 50926 (August 6, 2026).

### Summary of positions

I write in response to the proposal's information-barrier and
non-public-information questions only: requests (2), (5), (19), and (21) in
the request list for the proposed amendments to Commission Regulation 1.52;
requests (32) and (37) in the request list for proposed Commission
Regulations 38.852 and 37.1201; request (71)(a) in the request list for the
proposed derivatives clearing organization amendments; and the request for
comment "on whether reliance on existing voluntary practice is sufficient."
I take no position on whether any affiliated structure should be permitted,
prohibited, or conditioned; on the designated self-regulatory organization
framework, the proposed affiliate-DSRO prohibition, or the election
mechanism; on board composition, regulatory oversight committees, or
disciplinary panels; on the proposed prohibition on affiliate principal
trading firms or the conditioned market-maker exception, including
order-priority subordination and incentive parity; on the derivatives
clearing organization margin, default-management, and capital questions; on
the proposed futures commission merchant disclosure amendments; or on any
cost, burden, or capacity estimate. I have no supervisory, exchange,
intermediary, or clearing experience, and nothing in this comment supplies
any.

I am a software and formal-methods researcher; I study systems whose
correctness claims include claims about which actor may learn which
information. The positions are my own analysis as a commenter.

1. An information barrier is a claim about information flow: it asserts
   that named persons will not learn named classes of information. Stated
   with its parts explicit --- protected classes enumerated, actor sets
   named, each permitted flow listed with its condition, and the assurance
   mode declared --- that claim is specifiable and testable. Stated as an
   organizational assurance, the same claim can only be attested.
2. Barriers come in three assurance modes that differ in kind: prohibited
   by policy, prevented by technical control, and evidenced by access
   records. A barrier whose violation would be technically undetectable is
   a different obligation from one whose access events are logged, even
   when the two are written in the same sentence, and the rule should say
   which mode it requires where.
3. Examination should be able to consume access-structure evidence, not
   only policy documents. The proposed access and sharing prohibitions are
   checkable exactly to the extent that accesses are recorded and
   classifiable against the stated exception, and the proposed independent
   reviews and certifications are as strong as the evidence class beneath
   them.
4. The definition of "non-public information" should enumerate the
   protected categories, because a barrier obligation needs a named
   object; a dissemination-status test marks where protection ends, not
   what a barrier protects.

### A barrier is a statement of who may learn what (requests (19), (32), (37), (71)(a), and the voluntary-practice question)

The proposal's descriptions of current practice, and its requests for
more, are stated in the vocabulary of organizational assurance. Request
(37) asks exchanges to "describe the policies, procedures, governance
separations, information barriers, and disclosures currently in place";
request (71)(a) asks clearing organizations for the "policies, procedures,
rulebook provisions, organizational separations, and other measures
currently in place"; request (19) asks for the "existing policies,
procedures, information barriers, or technological controls" governing
access to an affiliate's non-public information. What the Commission will
receive in answer is prose about programs, and prose about programs is
hard to compare across entities and hard to examine against later facts.

The same content can be stated as an information-flow claim with four
explicit parts: which classes of information are protected --- the
proposal's own inventory, the "financial condition, customer activity,
risk profile, and proprietary trading" of member firms, is a list of class
names; which persons or organizational units must not learn them; which
flows are permitted and on what condition --- the proposed part 38
carve-out is such a permitted flow, and request (5) asks for exactly the
"additional specificity ... regarding the categories of non-public
information access and sharing permitted under the carve-out" that makes a
permitted flow testable; and in which assurance mode each barrier
operates. This is not a heavier obligation. It is the same obligation
stated so that two questions can be told apart: whether the entity
maintains the procedures, and whether the property the procedures assert
actually held during the period examined.

The proposed rule text already asserts the property form. Proposed
Commission Regulation 37.1201(b)(1) requires procedures such that a
facility's "applications, information and systems are maintained and
operated in a manner that prevents the sharing of non-public information
with any affiliate market participant" --- an information-flow property of
running systems. But the compliance object the rule creates is the
procedure, and the preamble's account of examinability is about the
procedure's existence: "The Commission can examine for compliance, and the
requirement places the burden on the exchange to design and implement
procedures suited to its particular structure and risk profile." Whether
an exchange has procedures is an objective inquiry; whether its systems in
fact prevented the sharing during the examination period is a different
objective inquiry, answerable only if the rule requires the systems to
produce the evidence. Hence my answer to request (32): the choice it
offers --- the principles-based procedures formulation versus enumerating
"additional specific procedures" in rule text --- omits a third form that
keeps the flexibility of the first, namely principles-based in content but
prescribed in form of statement and evidence. And my answer on voluntary
practice is the same point: the proposal's stated defect in voluntary
measures is that each "may be narrowed, modified, or discontinued" outside
the Commission's examination and enforcement processes. Codifying a
procedures obligation removes the discontinuation risk, but the content of
each barrier remains at the entity's discretion and off the record;
requiring the claim form --- who may learn what, in which mode --- is what
makes narrowing visible when it happens.

### Detectability separates barriers in kind (request (19))

Request (19) names "policies, procedures, information barriers, or
technological controls" in one series, but these differ in what a
violation leaves behind. A policy prohibition's violation leaves nothing
unless someone reports it or a separate process discovers it. A technical
prevention makes the prohibited access a non-event. Between them sits the
mode this comment most wants named: access that remains possible but is
recorded --- every access to a protected class by a person in a named set
leaves a record that the accessing party cannot suppress. The three modes
support different supervision: an undetectable-violation barrier can only
be attested; a logged barrier can be examined; a prevented barrier can be
tested. The proposal already draws this distinction for physical space
without naming it for systems --- the proposed guidance would require not
only "physical barriers" but "the ability of the designated contract
market to monitor for any instances where an affiliate market participant
has gained physical access." Monitoring for the violation is the recorded
mode. Its electronic analogue --- access to protected classes logged, with
the log beyond the reach of the parties whose conduct it evidences --- is
absent from the proposed rule text, and it is the single addition that
would most change what the rest of the proposed framework can see.

### Examination should consume access-structure evidence (requests (5) and (21))

Proposed Commission Regulation 1.52(c)(1)(i)(D) is drafted as an access
rule: a self-regulatory organization "may not access non-public
information of its affiliate futures commission merchant, except as
necessary to comply with its responsibilities and obligations as a
designated contract market under part 38 of this chapter," and is
"prohibited from sharing, directly or indirectly," supervisory-program
information with the affiliate "for any purpose," subject to the same
exception. An access rule with an exception is checkable in one way: each
access is recorded, and each access claimed under the exception is classed
to the part 38 obligation that necessitated it. That record is the
"additional specificity" request (5) should be read to seek --- not a
longer abstract list of permitted purposes, but the requirement that
invoking the carve-out be itself an auditable event carrying its stated
necessity. The proposed certifications inherit whatever evidence sits
beneath them: the regulatory service provider that must "review and
monitor the DCM's compliance with its conflicts of interest procedures"
and annually certify that "the DCM is operating in compliance with its
conflicts of interest procedures" can rest that certification on an access
record or on interviews and a policy binder, and a certification over
records is a verifiable summary while a certification over attestations is
an attestation. Request (21) asks whether the proposed definitions
"capture relationships or categories of information that differ in any
material respect from those addressed by existing arrangements" --- a
question an entity can answer mechanically if both its arrangements and
the definitions are stated over named classes, and only impressionistically
otherwise.

### The definition should name the protected classes (request (2))

Proposed Commission Regulation 1.52(a)(4) would define non-public
information as "information that has not been disseminated in a manner
which makes it generally available to the trading public," and request (2)
asks whether the concept should "be expressed in terms of materiality,
enumerated categories of information (e.g., customer positions, financial
condition, risk-management policies), as proposed, or based on some other
criteria." Enumerated categories, with the dissemination-status test
retained as the outer boundary. The reason is structural rather than a
matter of definitional taste: every substantive obligation in this seam is
a statement about flows of something, and the something must have a name
for the statement to be testable. *Customer positions may not flow from
the supervisory program to the affiliate* is checkable against systems and
records; *information not yet generally available may not flow* is
checkable only after a dispute establishes, item by item, what the phrase
covered. Materiality would be worse still: it makes membership in the
protected class a judgment reached after the flow has happened. The
categories the Commission has already written down --- customer positions,
financial condition, risk-management policies, proprietary-trading
information --- are the right kind of object.

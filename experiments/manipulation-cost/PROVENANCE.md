## Lineage boundary

The constant-product swap rule with a basis-point input fee, the quadratic for
the input that reaches a target reserve ratio, and the round-trip cost identity
are public mathematics, derived freshly in `src/pool.rs` from the invariant and
documented there rather than cited to an implementation. The bucket-grid mean is
the ordinary time-weighted statistic over equal-duration buckets.

No source code, fixture, generated artifact, constant table, theorem text, or
serialization format was copied from Breadstuffs, Minidregg, Leanuweave, or
Dragon's Clutch. The sampling-grid vocabulary matches the shape of the Dragon's
Clutch observation accumulator by description, in the terms the program's own
architecture documents already state; no accumulator code was read into this
crate and this crate does not claim clean-room status.

## What is asserted and what is not

VERIFIED, at exactly the bounds the tests state: the arithmetic of this model.
Every number in `vectors/v1.txt` and `vectors/v2-offhours.txt` is an exact
integer computed from stated synthetic parameters and is reproducible
byte-for-byte.

PROPOSED: that a number of this shape belongs in a surveillance picture.

Not asserted, in any form: that these numbers describe, approximate, bound, or
predict the cost of manipulating any real venue, pool, oracle, index, or
contract. The recovery model, and the list of effects it omits, is stated in
full in `src/pool.rs` and repeated in the README. The results are a lower bound
under those assumptions.

Additionally not asserted for the off-hours table: that any real session is thin
by any of these ratios, that any real window straddles a thin session in any of
these proportions, or that the reported cost is the cheapest attack available.
The `cost` column is the cost of one stated schedule --- an attack mounted from
inside the thin session --- and the corpus header names the strategies it does
not price, each of which would lower the figure. A retention ratio above 10000
records that the thin session offered that schedule no discount; it does not say
a design is dearer to attack than `vectors/v1.txt` already reports, because the
same adversary may attack the normal session instead.

byte-for-byte against `vectors/v1.txt` by a unit test. Two independent
computations cross-check each other: exact integer bisection against the

## Receiver checks: the off-hours table

The corpus is generated by `session::render_vectors_v2()` and checked
byte-for-byte against `vectors/v2-offhours.txt` by a unit test. It is anchored to
the first table in two independent ways: every row's `cost_v1` column is read out
of the checked-in `vectors/v1.txt` and compared, and every row's `cost` is
compared against `table::row()` evaluated at that row's thin depth and effective
hold count --- which at depth ratio 1/1, for the 864 rows whose session carries
the design's whole exposure, reproduces the v1 cost and capital exactly. One row
is re-derived by hand in `tests/offhours.rs`, every integer of it, including the
one-unit margin that decides the excursion size and the reversal that sets the
cost.
